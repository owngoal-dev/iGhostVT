iGhostVT 1.5.0 lets a Mac install its own updates — from its menu, from the command line, or from a paired iPhone or iPad — keeps a host awake while you use it from another device, and starts a new release line: it connects only to devices on 1.5.

- Updating iGhostVT on a Mac meant downloading a zip, moving it into Applications and opening it again. Check for Updates… now installs the update itself: the Mac downloads the notarized release, installs it only if its signature is valid and comes from the same developer team as the copy you have, and relaunches iGhostVT. Because the team stays the same, macOS keeps every permission you granted, such as access to your Documents folder or the local network, and does not ask again. Updating ends the terminals open on that Mac. A copy that cannot update itself, such as one you built yourself, still downloads the zip as before.
- A Mac you use from another device can now be updated from that device. On an iPhone or iPad, open Settings ▸ Remote Access, choose the Mac and tap Check for Update; on another Mac, use the Check for Update button beside it. On the Mac itself, `ighostvt-cli update` does the same from a terminal, and `ighostvt-cli update --check` only says whether there is one.
- A Mac or a jailbroken device could go to sleep while you were working on it from another device, dropping your terminals halfway through a command or a file transfer. The host now stays awake for five minutes after the last time a device typed into a terminal, opened one or sent a file, and for up to half an hour while a command you started is still printing. The display still sleeps, and closing a Mac's lid still puts it to sleep.
- On an iPhone or iPad, the screen could lock during a long `rz`/`sz` transfer or while a dropped file was being copied to another device, and the transfer stopped. The screen now stays on until the transfer ends.
- A device that went to sleep on the same network as its host could leave the host waiting on the connection. The host now notices within 30 seconds, whether the device is connected directly or through a relay, and the device reconnects to the same terminals when it wakes.
- After another device disconnected while a lot of output was waiting to be sent to it, the terminals on the host could pause for a moment. Output for a device that is gone is now thrown away at once.
- The installer `mac-install.sh` now leaves iGhostVT owned by the user it is installed for and clears any download marks from it, so the app can update itself later.
- Devices on different releases cannot connect to each other. Update every device to 1.5.0 together: iGhostVT on your Macs and jailbroken devices, and Ghost Remote on your other iPhones and iPads.

Choose `iphoneos-arm64e` for roothide, `iphoneos-arm64` for a rootless bootstrap, `iGhostVT-1.5.0-macos-notarized.zip` for a Mac — it is signed and notarized, so it opens without clearing quarantine, and updates itself from now on — or `GhostRemote-1.5.0.ipa` for an iPhone or iPad without custom firmware, which your sideloading app signs as it installs it. To run the relay, use the attached `compose.yml`; the image is `ghcr.io/owngoal-dev/ighostvt-relay`, and the digest is in `relay-image.txt`. SHA-256 sums are in `SHA256SUMS`, and the Mac app's in `SHA256SUMS.macos`.
